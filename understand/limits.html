    Scope, limitations and roadmap¶

    These pages document the released package, meidnet 2.3.1. MEIDNet Matter, the applied companion, runs a development snapshot of the engine and states the measured limits of its five case studies on its Scope and limits page.

    Limitations¶

    • A candidate is a proposal. It is a composition placed on a family prototype, with properties predicted by the model. Nothing in the package runs DFT. meidnet screen relaxes candidates with MACE-MP-0 and applies a formation-energy filter (at most 0.10 eV/atom against the elemental phases): a first filter, not stability on the convex hull (screening).
    • The published model knows one chemistry. It was trained on Perov-5, its test split included: cubic ABX₃ cells of five atoms whose anion sites hold oxygen and nitrogen, with fluorine and sulfur only in mixed sets. Perov-5 has no Cl, Br, I or Se, and La is its only lanthanide, so halide and lanthanide candidates from this model are extrapolations; the demo and the Studio nevertheless start from the halide variant.
    • Its property predictions are rough. On Perov-5's test split, which was part of its training data, the band gap read from a structure alone misses by 2.79 eV on average, against 0.17 eV for always predicting the training mean (1.68 against 2.14 eV on the non-zero gaps); the formation enthalpy does better than the mean (0.40 against 0.57 eV/atom). See the Perov-5 leaderboard.
    • What has been validated. Only Perov-5 has a benchmark protocol. Its inverse-design score combines the formation-energy filter, uniqueness, novelty and a DFT check that is possible only where Perov-5 holds the same sites, in practice for oxides: the published model met the band-gap target in 1 of 18 such checks, and its ΔHf·U·N (0.611) is below both baselines (0.778 and 0.852). MP-20 and Carbon-24 have no protocol yet. Nothing has been tested experimentally.
    • Which crystal structures. Both built-in families are cubic: Pm-3m ABX₃, and Fm-3m A₂BB′X₆ in its rhombohedral primitive cell. A family file may use a general cell (a, b, c, alpha, beta, gamma); it is scaled from one edge with its shape fixed, needs a model trained on structures of that prototype, and its rules must be written for it, since the tolerance and octahedral factors are perovskite rules. No non-cubic family ships or has been tested, and the hosted Studio takes the built-in families only.
    • The rules are approximations. The tolerance and octahedral factors use ionic radii averaged over every oxidation state pymatgen lists, which shrinks every anion except oxygen: with the built-in bounds, CsPbBr₃ (octahedral factor 1.27) and CsPbCl₃ (1.44) fail the 0.90 limit. Read halide and nitride results with this in mind; a fix will use the radius of the oxidation state that balances charge.

    What a dataset needs¶

    What the rows contain matters more than how many there are. Measured on MEIDNet Matter's case studies:

    to do this what it needs measured
    predict a property from structure a well-posed property: continuous, few zero values, few materials sharing one property profile 166 training structures read a band gap with r 0.97 and a 0.65 eV error (34 test structures); 897 with 35 % zero gaps fell short on telling metals from non-metals
    screen an enumerated family data that fit one family, and a property model that passes 166 to 897 training structures
    generate one prototype, and hundreds of distinct compositions per element for the decoder Perov-5 generates; data with 11 to 48 compositions per element went to screening

    A few hundred structures train a property model, not a generator. The package's own checks: meidnet check warns below 200 usable rows; the training report's note below 500 training materials is a rule of thumb, not a measurement; training needs at least batch_size materials (16 by default); the validation split is a random 10 %, and there is none below 10 rows. The published model cannot be fine-tuned on new properties yet, so a small dataset trains a model from scratch. What data do I need?

    Current capabilities¶
    Structures any CIF with up to max_sites atoms (default 20); any elements
    Properties any number of scalar columns
    Training your data, your properties; the published settings by default
    Generation prototype families: a fixed arrangement of sites whose occupants and cell size are chosen. Built in: cubic ABX₃, A₂BB′X₆; write your own in YAML
    Rules charge balance, tolerance and octahedral factors, bond windows, minimum distance, predicted-property windows, element filters, and your own Python rules
    Explanations reports for data, training and generation; the Studio for live what-if

    On the hosted Studio: up to 1,500 rows and 8 MB per upload, 30 training epochs, 20 atoms per cell (the Studio).

    Not yet supported¶

    • New atomic arrangements. The decoder's positions are not used to create geometry; candidates are compositions placed on the family prototype. Generating free geometry needs a decoder trained to produce it (the published decoder receives the true positions during training). MEIDNet Matter runs a development snapshot of the engine, meidnet 2.4.0.dev4, whose symmetry decoder, trained on MP-20, builds new cells without a family and samples at the target's anchor instead of optimising the latent point. It is not in a release of this package yet; its measured limits (on MP-20 more than half of the relaxed cells collapse, and a delivered gap is good to about ±0.7 eV) are on Matter's Scope and limits page.
    • Spectra, images, text as modalities. The property modality is a vector of scalars. Binned XRD or DOS could be added as a vector modality with its own encoder; it is designed for but not implemented.
    • Large cells. max_sites can be raised, but memory and time grow with the square of it, and the published model was trained on 5-atom cells.
    • Fine-tuning the published model on new properties. Its property head has two outputs; train a new model.

    Roadmap (in order of likely value)¶

    1. Vector modalities (XRD, DOS) with a shared-latent encoder each.
    2. A training.init_from option to continue training from a checkpoint with the same properties.
    3. A geometry-generating decoder variant (trained from prototype positions instead of true positions — model.decoder_coordinate_input: prototype is the first step and is available as an experimental setting).
    4. More built-in families (spinel, Heusler, rocksalt/zincblende, MXene M₂XT₂).
    5. Ionic radii from the charge-balancing oxidation state in the tolerance and octahedral factors.

    Contributions of family files and rules are the easiest way to help; see the GitHub repository.
